2. HDR selection model

For a pixel before clipping, the recorded digital value can be represented as:

D(x,y) = B + G · η · Φ(x,y) · t + n(x,y),   0 ≤ D ≤ Dmax = 65,535

Here B is the offset/background, G is the fixed camera gain, η is system conversion efficiency, Φ is photon flux, t is exposure time, and n is noise. An ND filter changes flux by:

Φafter ND = Φbefore ND · 10−OD

A valid HDR set contains: (i) a short exposure that preserves relevant bright PSF/diffraction content, (ii) a middle exposure overlapping the short and long bands, and (iii) a long exposure that raises dim DOE features above noise. Long-band clipping is permissible only when the short band contains the clipped relevant information.

tshort = 13.9 ms;   tmid = 50 ms;   tlong candidate ∈ {125 ms, 250 ms}

Screening statistics used in the automated report are the worst per-frame 99.9th percentile and the fraction of pixels at Dmax. They are screening evidence only. Final acceptance must inspect whether clipped pixels occur in the scientifically relevant PSF/diffraction region.

3. Final bracket decisions

The fixed-ND configuration made 13.9 ms completely unclipped in the final blue tests for both cells and both DOE states. The 250 ms band raised dim with-DOE content while allowing the short band to preserve bright structure. A three-band bracket is selected to protect scan time.

4. Timing model

Tpass = Nposition · Nz · [Σi=1..b(ti + ocamera,i) + tz-settle] + TXY + Twrite

Measured paired no-DOE/with-DOE time per XY position is 10.4 seconds for all or green and 16.6–16.7 seconds for blue or red. Thus the complete four-color acquisition costs 54.10 seconds/position for cell2 and 54.32 seconds/position for cell5. These values include camera readout and TIFF writing, but exclude operator transitions.
